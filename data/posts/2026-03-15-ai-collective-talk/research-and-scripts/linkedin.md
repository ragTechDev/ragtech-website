Women in AI aren't being left behind. We're being more careful.

I spoke at AI Collective Singapore yesterday for International Women's Day, and this is the tension I kept coming back to: as someone in tech who builds with AI every single day, I feel pulled in two directions. AI is a genuine force multiplier. But I can't ignore what it costs: the art scraped without consent, the biases baked in, the jobs being displaced.

So how do you champion a tool you have serious concerns about?

My answer is what I call intentional AI. Every use is a deliberate choice, not a reflex. In practice that means: I've never used image or video generation (and I post visual content daily). Everything defaults to code. I never generate the same thing twice, I build a system instead. All my thinking is mine, AI only cleans up the language.

And if you look at who's doing the most critical work in the field right now, it's disproportionately women. Joy Buolamwini. Timnit Gebru. Frances Haugen. Kate Crawford. Fei-Fei Li. Karen Hao. Women who built institutions, published research that made powerful companies uncomfortable, and took real professional risks to make AI accountable.

We're not absent from AI. We're overrepresented in the rooms where accountability is being demanded.
