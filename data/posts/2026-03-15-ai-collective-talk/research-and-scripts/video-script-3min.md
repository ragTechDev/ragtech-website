# 3-Minute Video Script: Women in AI: The Dilemma Nobody Talks About

**Format:** Talking head to camera, short-form
**Target length:** ~3 minutes (~450 words at natural speaking pace)
**For:** ragTech social media (Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts / TikTok)

---

Get ready with me for a talk I'm giving at AI Collective Singapore, because I want to answer something directly.

I've been called an AI hater. The argument goes: you point out problems with AI, therefore you're against it, and you're probably just scared it's going to take your job. I'm a software engineer, I co-host a tech podcast called ragTech, and yes, I talk a lot about AI's pitfalls on here.

But that framing is a false binary. Either you're a cheerleader or you're a hater. Either you adopt everything uncritically or you're standing in the way of progress. I reject that completely.

Here's a stat. Women are 16 percentage points less likely to use AI at work than men in the same job. The usual headline: women are being left behind. But when you look at *why*, women cite privacy concerns, bias, hallucinations, job displacement. They're not absent. They're skeptical. And when a technology has already been shown to recommend lower salaries for women and misidentify women of color at significantly higher rates, that skepticism is not ignorance. It's a reasonable response to documented harm.

I'm not a hater. I'm an intentional user. And there's a meaningful difference.

I have a genuine dilemma. On one side, AI is a real force multiplier. I use it every day, across coding, content, automations, all of it. On the other side, I can't ignore what it costs. Artists scraped without consent. Biases that hurt women and minorities. Jobs displaced quietly, often from the people who can least afford it.

How do you champion a tool you have serious concerns about?

My answer: you use it deliberately. Not as a reflex, not as a default. A conscious decision every time, because you've weighed the benefit against the cost.

What that looks like for me: I've never used image or video generation, not once, even though I post visual content every day. I generate graphics through code instead. SVGs, HTML. Because image models are trained on artists who didn't consent, and I won't benefit from that when alternatives exist.

And I never let AI do my thinking. Every argument started in my head. AI helps me sharpen the words. It doesn't replace the work.

That's what I mean by using it intentionally AI. Not hating. Not cheerleading. Using it with your eyes open.

Tonight I'm also talking about the women leading responsible AI at the highest level. Women who built institutions, published research that made powerful companies uncomfortable, and paid a real professional price for it. They're not haters either. They're the most important practitioners in the field right now.

See you tonight.
