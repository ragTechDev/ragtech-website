# Teleprompter Script: What If Childcare Was Paid Like NS?
**Format:** Short-form vertical video (Reels/TikTok/Shorts)
**Duration:** ~2.5 min
**Speaker:** Natasha

---

Singapore's fertility rate just fell to 0.87. I'm a software engineer at ragTech, and I wanted to understand this problem the way I understand most problems: with data. So I ran a data science experiment. I had a hypothesis, I pulled real numbers from Singapore's open data portal, and I tested it. This is what I found.

The hypothesis: what if we treated parenthood like national service?

In data science, a hypothesis is just a question you're trying to answer with numbers. Mine was: Singapore's birth rate is falling, which means fewer men entering NS each year. Could the money saved from smaller NS cohorts fund a parental leave scheme instead?

Before I get to the numbers, here's the logic behind the hypothesis. NS is a 2-year sacrifice we pay men to make for national security. Parenthood is also a 2-year sacrifice, with sleepless nights, career setbacks, financial stress, and a next generation of taxpayers and workers as the output. If one is worth paying for, why not the other?

The proposal: 400 days of fully paid parental leave. Here's why 400 days. Every NSman has 10 reservist cycles, up to 40 days each. 10 times 40 is 400 days of paid duty we already fund. So this number isn't pulled from nowhere.

Now, step one of any data experiment: get your data. I used three datasets from data.gov.sg: births by year, NS allowance rates, and median income. All publicly available. All free.

Step two: calculate. The full cost of NS per male isn't just the 2 years of NSF allowance. You have to add 400 days of reservist make-up pay at civilian salary rates. That's about $114,000 per man over his NS career. At the national level, Singapore spends roughly $2.4 billion a year on NS personnel.

Step three: compare. A parental leave scheme at NSF rates for all 30,000 births in 2025 would cost $780 million. One-third of what we spend on NS. Per person: $26,000 per parent versus $114,000 per NSman. 23 cents for every dollar.

The verdict: the data supports the hypothesis. The money is already there as a precedent. And if we apply the same legal framework, NS-style penalties for employers who discriminate against parents on leave, the same way workplaces already accept men leaving for reservist 40 days a year for 10 years, then the culture is achievable too. Add flexible WFH when parents return to work, and this is not radical. It's just consistent.
