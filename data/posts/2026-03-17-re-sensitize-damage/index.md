Nat:
To re-sensitize means to understand what you have been desensitized from in the first place. Many people are unaware of how social media algorithms and the frequent use of AI has affected them, and do not have breathing space away from the said technology to let their nervous systems and bodies go back to a baseline. 

I'd say the most effective desensitization technique is first to take an effective break from it and reduce time spent on it. While a complete break is difficult, what users can first do is to identify the amount of time spent daily on their devices. Then from there, try to find pockets of time to replace usage with something else. For me, I try to replace scrolling with reading. When I find myself scrolling out of boredom while waiting, I try to switch that to reading instead. This means making books equally as accessible as phones to you during these liminal waiting times. Are you often scrolling before falling asleep? Put a book on your night stand. Are you often scrolling while on the toilet? Put another book or a magazine by your sink. Are you often scrolling while eating? Put a book on your dining table. It doesn't have to be reading of course, it could be crocheting, beading, sketching - anything analogue. And make it as incentivizing as scrolling on social media! Social media tends to trigger dopamine when we scroll and find something that we like. We can trigger dopamine too with our habits - I found that keeping track of how many pages I read in a day as a progress indicator (I used an app initially to record my book reading progress) helped me find reading as incentivizing as scrollig on social media. Within a week I had already built up that habit, and I didn't need the app anymore! So those are very practical, little tips to reduce time on the screen and replace it with another habit.

Once you spend less time on the screen, you would have allowed your hormones to get back to a lower baseline. No longer are you continuously exposed to violence that keep your cortisol levels at a high. No longer are you scrolling on social media looking for the next dopamine hit, such that your attention span is worn down. And no longer do you have heightened levels of anxiety from the constant, subconscious status marking and social comparison when on social media platforms. It's a small change, but it has a huge difference in your quality of life. You'd also find yourself being more present with your surroundings, and more present to process emotions that previously did not have an avenue when triggered through social media. Take your time to sit with those feelings rather than jump to the next feel-good video. Have discussions with a person in real life about how they felt about the same topic. Find validation that somebody else feels the same way. Realize that you aren't alone and that your feelings are valid and they matter.

Perhaps what most people struggle with is finding a person to talk about these feelings with. It sounds silly to go up to someone and ask them, hey, how do you feel about the war in XXX? You're afraid of your feelings of anxiety or your opinion being invalidated, and its safer to just ignore your feelings or to express them on social media as comments. To that, I'd say you shouldn't be afraid of bringing up these topics to those close to you. Preface it by saying that you've been feeling overwhelmed, and would just like to talk about it with someone. Anyone who cares about you should be able to give you that space. They don't have to be interested in the same topic to listen to you, and they can also learn about the topic through you. So go ahead, start that conversation with someone you know cares about you. Could be your parent, partner or friend. And you might be surprised where that conversation takes you.

Beyond conversations, I also find reading long-form content to be helpful in rebuilding my appetite for deeper thinking. Pick something to be obsessed with that is long-form - could be a new drama, a documentary, a book, a YouTube video or Spotify podcast. Replace short-form tidbits with long-form content. That helps rebuild that sensitivity as it gives you extended exposure and space to one topic, where you can let your feelings completely play out and fully process your thoughts on one topic rather than skipping to the next.
