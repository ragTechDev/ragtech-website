# 3-Minute Video Script: Women in AI: The Dilemma Nobody Talks About

**Format:** Talking head to camera, short-form
**Target length:** ~3 minutes (~450 words at natural speaking pace)
**For:** ragTech social media (Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts / TikTok)

---

[OPEN on camera, direct address]

There's this tension a lot of women in tech feel but don't say out loud. We know AI can help us do so much more. But we also know the real costs: the biases, the job displacement, the art scraped without consent. How do you champion a tool you have serious concerns about?

That's a question a lot of AI content doesn't ask. So let me try to answer it.

---

[BEAT]

First, a stat. In the US, 50% of men use popular AI tools. Only 37% of women do. And that gap exists even within the exact same jobs.

The usual take: women are being left behind.

But women are more likely to cite privacy concerns. To worry about bias and hallucinations. To think harder about who gets displaced. That's not being left behind. That's being more informed.

---

[SECTION 2: Intentional AI]

My approach is what I call intentional AI. Every single time I use AI, it's a deliberate choice. Not a reflex.

Three rules I follow:

One: I have never used image or video generation. Not once. I post visual content every day, but I use code to generate graphics instead. Lighter, and no artists scraped for it.

Two: I never generate the same thing twice. If I'm asking AI to do the same task on repeat, I build a system for it. Automate once, run forever. That's the engineering mindset.

Three: all my thinking is mine. AI cleans up my language. It never does my thinking. Every opinion, every argument in my writing started in my own head.

---

[SECTION 3: Women in AI Ethics]

And about that 22% statistic, women making up only 22% of AI professionals globally.

Look at who is actually doing the ethical work in this field.

Joy Buolamwini exposed racial and gender bias in facial recognition. Timnit Gebru got fired from Google for co-authoring a paper about the risks of large language models. Frances Haugen blew the whistle on Facebook at great personal risk. Fei-Fei Li built AI4ALL. Kate Crawford literally wrote the book on AI's hidden costs.

Women are not absent from AI. We are disproportionately in the rooms where accountability is being demanded.

---

[CLOSE]

So my answer to the dilemma? Use AI intentionally, critically, and with your community in mind.

Not because you have to. Because you've decided to.
