## THE PROTEST (0:15-0:45)

**[ON SCREEN: Map showing Gelang Patah location]**

**NATASHA:**  
On February 7th, over 50 residents protested outside a data center construction site in Gelang Patah. This was Malaysia's first-ever data center protest.

These weren't tech activists or environmentalists—just everyday people who couldn't dry their laundry outside anymore because of construction dust, whose air purifiers were constantly showing dangerous air quality levels.

But here's the real issue: this facility will consume up to 50 million liters of water per day. That's enough to fill 20 Olympic-sized swimming pools. Every. Single. Day.

---

## THE TECH REALITY (0:45-1:15)

**[ON SCREEN: Animation showing data center water usage]**

**NATASHA:**  
You might be thinking: "Why not just use seawater? We're near the ocean!"

The thing is, data centers need **potable water**. Drinkable water. The same water you and I use.

Why? Because seawater corrodes pipes, causes biological buildup, and would require expensive desalination. So data centers compete directly with communities for freshwater.

Every time you use ChatGPT, stream Netflix, or upload to the cloud—there's physical infrastructure somewhere consuming massive resources.

---

## WHY MALAYSIA AND SINGAPORE? (1:45-2:00)

**[ON SCREEN: Map of Southeast Asia with Singapore-Malaysia highlighted]**

**NATASHA:**  
So why build here? Two words: **latency** and **location**.

Southeast Asia has 700 million people. If your servers are in the US, every click adds 200-300 milliseconds of lag. For engineers deploying apps, you need data centers close to your users.

Singapore has strict environmental regulations. Malaysia offers the same proximity with lower costs and fewer rules. That's why tech giants like Amazon and ByteDance are racing to build in Johor.

## THE GLOBAL MOVEMENT (2:00-2:20)

**[ON SCREEN: Quick cuts of protest locations]**

**NATASHA:**  
And Johor isn't alone. In Monterey Park, California, just five residents stopped a massive data center in six weeks. 

From March to June 2025, communities worldwide cancelled **$98 billion worth of data center projects**. Two-thirds of targeted projects were halted.

This is happening because ordinary people are learning how tech actually works—and demanding accountability.
