# Teleprompter Script: What If Childcare Was Paid Like NS?
**Format:** Short-form vertical video (Reels/TikTok/Shorts)
**Duration:** ~3 min
**Speaker:** Natasha

---

Singapore's fertility rate just fell to 0.87. And I have a radical hypothesis to help change that: what if we treated parenthood like national service? Since I'm a software engineer with my own tech podcast, ragTech, let's test this hypothesis with data science.

In data science, a hypothesis is just a question you answer with numbers. Mine: birth rates are falling, so NS intakes are shrinking. Could the savings fund a parental leave scheme instead?

The logic: NS is a 2-year sacrifice we pay men to make for national security. Parenthood is the same kind of sacrifice with the same kind of output. If one is worth paying for, why not the other?

The proposal: 400 days of fully paid parental leave instead of the current 4 months. Why 400? Every NSman does 10 reservist cycles, up to 40 days each. 10 times 40 is 400 days of paid duty we already fund.

Step one: get your data. I used three datasets from data.gov.sg: births by year, NS allowance rates, and median income.

Step two: calculate. NS cost per male isn't just the 2 years of NSF allowance. There's also reservist make-up pay: the government reimburses employers at the NSman's full civilian salary rate, 40 days a year, for 10 years. That's $88,600 per man for reservist alone. Total lifetime NS cost per male: $114,000. Nationally: $2.4 billion a year.

Here's what the data showed that I didn't expect: NS is already getting cheaper because birth rates are falling. Peak was $2.74 billion in 2018. By 2025 it's down to $2.39 billion. That's $350 million in savings a year, already happening.

Step three: compare. A parental leave scheme at NSF rates for all 30,000 births in 2025 would cost $780 million. One-third of what we spend on NS. Per person: $26,000 per parent versus $114,000 per NSman. 23 cents for every dollar.

The verdict: the data supports the hypothesis. That $350 million in savings covers 45% of the parental leave scheme already. And if births recover because of the policy, the remaining cost shrinks further. It is self-correcting.

Apply the same legal framework: under the Enlistment Act, firing someone for NS obligations means fines and jail time. Apply the same to parental leave. Workplaces already plan around men leaving for reservist 40 days a year for 10 years. If they can do that, they can handle a mother on parental leave. Add flexible WFH when she returns, and this is not radical. It is just consistency.

I'm posting this following International Women's Day, because this is exactly the conversation we should be having. Not just celebrating women in the workforce, but asking what it actually costs to become a mother, and whether we're willing to share that cost the way we share the cost of defence. The data says we can. The question is whether we will.
