**Duration:** ~3 minutes

Yesterday, I covered Malaysia's first data center protest in Johor. I did more research on Zdata, the company behind it, and found even more red flags.

I'm Natasha, a software engineer and co-host of ragTech.

On February 11th, Zdata claimed reports about dust and water concerns were "inaccurate." But their own loan application to the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank states their projects will have "heavy requirements of water and electricity" and generate "dusts and particulates" during construction.

So in their loan application, they acknowledge these impacts. But when residents protest? Suddenly it's "inaccurate." That's a credibility problem.

The facility is less than one kilometer from homes—unusual for data centers. Officials won't disclose what tier it is, despite Johor halting Tier 1 and 2 approvals because they consume 50 million liters of water daily. Malaysia's data center water consumption is projected to hit 93 billion liters by 2030.

In 2025, authorities issued a stop-work order for poor construction standards. When work resumed, problems continued.

Here's the financing red flag: Zdata applied for a 40 million dollar loan and was seeking 500 million in private debt—both while actively building. You secure financing before breaking ground, not during construction. This signals serious capitalization issues.

Their loan commits them to a Grievance Redress Mechanism for community concerns. Yet when 50 residents protested, they got a defensive PR statement. No grievance mechanism. No engagement. If it existed, the protest might never have happened.

Compare this to Equinix, who engages communities a year before construction. Other companies use sustainable cooling, renewable energy, and transparency. These are industry best practices, not impossible standards.

Zdata's corner-cutting created broken trust, regulatory scrutiny, and reputational damage. All avoidable.

I'm not anti-AI. I'm a software engineer excited about what AI can do. But I have a responsibility to advocate for doing it right. We've seen decades of irresponsible tech—social media harming mental health, biased algorithms, privacy violations, displaced communities.

I'm not a doomsayer scared of layoffs. I'm putting my knowledge to use.

The Zdata project is not representative of all data centers. It's an example of how not to do it. And as someone who builds technology, I refuse to stay silent when I see it done irresponsibly.

Follow ragTech for more tech explainers that matter. I'm Natasha—tech literacy is power.
